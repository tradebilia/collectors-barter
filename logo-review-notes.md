# Tradebilia Logo Review Notes

The current **Tradebilia** logo asset is a horizontal lockup shown inside a larger image field. The logo itself combines a multicolor circular emblem made from angled shard-like segments, a thin vertical divider, the **TRADEBILIA** wordmark in bold white uppercase lettering, and the subtitle **Collectors Trading Exchange** in smaller white type. The surrounding deep navy area should be treated as presentation background around the exported image, not as part of the logo mark itself.

| Aspect | Current observation | Practical implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Symbol | The circular icon is distinctive and energetic, with multiple bright colors around a dark center. | It feels collectible-focused and memorable, but the small internal shapes can lose clarity at reduced sizes. |
| Wordmark | The main wordmark is bold, clean, and highly legible on dark backgrounds. | It works well as a hero lockup, especially when enough horizontal space is available. |
| Subtitle | The subtitle is refined but much smaller than the main mark. | It becomes difficult to read when the full lockup is scaled down. |
| Composition | The asset is built as a wide horizontal banner rather than a square or transparent logo package. | It is strong for hero sections, but less flexible when used in tight spaces like circular badges, compact nav bars, or small banner identity blocks. |
| Background | The exported image currently includes surrounding dark navy presentation space around the logo. | That surrounding field should not be treated as part of the logo itself, and homepage presentation can separate the mark from the backdrop more cleanly. |

At the site level, the brand currently appears in two main ways. In some places the full horizontal lockup is used directly, while in the Sports Cards banner the emblem is separated into a circular crop and paired with a text treatment rebuilt in code. That split approach is workable, but it suggests the brand may need a more explicit system consisting of a standalone symbol, a standalone wordmark, and a combined lockup.

# Collectors Forum: Comparative Layout Research

## Current observation

The existing Forum page places a wide search input immediately below category chips and directly above sort chips. On desktop, it resembles the first field of a composer rather than a navigation utility. On phone, it becomes a large bordered card inserted between category selection and the discussion feed; the hierarchy is dense, and a frequent Facebook Group or Reddit visitor has to parse too many similarly weighted controls before reaching topics.

## Familiar patterns worth adopting

Facebook Groups positions search as a group utility rather than the primary discussion action. Its official help instructs people to select a group, use Search, enter names or keywords found in posts or comments, then choose filters from a separate left-side area.[1]

Reddit supports community-scoped search and distinguishes search filtering/sorting from its post-composer action.[2] Its help documentation separately identifies community navigation, search/filter/sort, post and comment creation, image comments, and moderation tools.[2]

## Recommendation for Tradebilia

Use a collector-community layout rather than duplicating either platform. The category/subcategory trail should become the forum’s community navigation. Place **Search discussions** as a compact header utility aligned with the section title on desktop, and a narrow full-width utility immediately below the heading on phone. Do not position it in a large standalone panel.

Reserve the clear primary action for **Start a discussion**. On desktop, place it at the top right opposite the Forum Topics heading. On phone, use a full-width action directly under the topic heading and above search. Keep feed controls below this: a lightweight `Newest | Popular | Most replies` sort row and an `All | Unanswered | Recently active` filter menu or compact chip row. The actual discussion feed should begin immediately after these controls.

This keeps discovery available, preserves the Facebook-style separation of search from posting, and uses the Reddit-style distinction among community navigation, feed filtering, and authoring.

# Category Rollout Audit

The current Sports Cards page now functions as the clearest benchmark for the broader category rollout. Its strongest reusable qualities are the practical left rail, the tighter summary cards, the more literal spotlight-card rhythm, and the stronger empty-state behavior. The remaining category pages already inherit the shared structure and category-specific content definitions, but they still need selective visual refinements and validation so the rollout feels deliberate rather than merely templated.

| Category | Current shared intent | Main rollout opportunity | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Comics | Editorial convention-wall tone with key-issue emphasis | Make the spotlight row feel as inventory-like and grounded as the refined Sports Cards card-table treatment | High |
| Vintage Toys | Museum-like showroom with boxed nostalgia | Increase the object-first presentation so carded and boxed toys read more clearly as collectible inventory | High |
| Video Games | Bright retro aisle with platform energy | Tighten the spotlight cards so sealed and graded games feel less poster-like and more shelf-ready | High |
| Stamps | Archival collector-catalog tone | Preserve the quiet identity while strengthening the literal catalog-card rhythm in the highlight row | Medium |
| Coins | Formal cabinet-style luxury presentation | Improve the inventory-card clarity so numismatic objects read more like premium cabinet pulls than editorial tiles | Medium |
| Pokemon | Bright chase-card energy | Translate the Sports Cards rhythm into a more playful but still slab-focused card grid | High |
| Movies | Marquee memorabilia and premiere-night tone | Keep cinematic atmosphere while making posters and props feel more browseable and less cinematic-banner-driven | Medium |
| Autographs | Provenance-heavy gallery mood | Improve object framing so signed items read more like trusted listings than showcase panels | Medium |
| Disney Pins | Playful premium enamel-display tone | Apply the benchmark rhythm with brighter, smaller-object framing so the pin cards feel collectible and crisp | Medium |

The most efficient next rollout order is **Comics**, **Pokemon**, **Vintage Toys**, and **Video Games** first, because those categories can benefit the most from the Sports Cards benchmark improvements without losing their distinct identities. The remaining archival or provenance-heavy categories should follow once that more literal object-card treatment is stable across the visually louder categories.

## Validation snapshot: Comics

The Comics page now inherits the shared **collector spotlight** treatment successfully. The lower section renders as a literal three-card collectible lane instead of a looser editorial block, and the objects read clearly enough to support the rollout direction established by Sports Cards. The crimson identity still differentiates Comics from Sports Cards, but the benchmark rhythm is now visibly consistent across the two categories.

## Validation snapshot: Pokemon

The Pokemon page also renders the shared **collector spotlight** section correctly, confirming that the broader rollout is not limited to Sports Cards and Comics. The brighter yellow-and-cobalt identity remains distinct, while the new spotlight row still reads as collectible inventory instead of a loose editorial band. This gives stronger confidence that the shared benchmark treatment can scale across additional high-energy categories without flattening their personalities.

## Validation snapshot: Vintage Toys

The Vintage Toys page preserves a distinct silver-olive museum tone while still rendering the shared **collector spotlight** structure, summary block, and empty-state behavior cleanly. The page feels consistent with the benchmark rollout, and no structural issue stands out beyond the still-open need to complete the same browser check across the rest of the remaining categories.

## Validation snapshot: Video Games

The Video Games page keeps its neon arcade identity while still following the shared benchmark layout, including the summary support area, spotlight lanes, and stronger empty-state guidance. The category-specific voice remains intact, so the page currently reads as a successful adaptation of the benchmark rather than a copy of Sports Cards.
